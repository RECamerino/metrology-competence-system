# Rubric — ARC-0005, Witnessed measurement-method test on a weighing instrument

Rubric-scored throughout, by a witness present for the whole test. **The
majority of the weight is on what the witness saw, not on what the candidate
handed over**, and that is the point of the archetype rather than an accident of
it.

The archetype spans the measurement methods cg-18 groups together. The
components below apply to every test in that family; what the witness is looking
for under each is stated on the **binding**, because it is a property of the
procedure and the binding is where the procedure is chosen.

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| The load was manipulated as the method requires | 25% |
| The instrument's automatic zero aids were configured as the method requires | 20% |
| Handling held constant across the readings | 15% |
| Indications recorded correctly, and reduced with the right arithmetic | 25% |
| Decisions the record would not otherwise show, stated | 15% |

## The first three components cannot be scored retrospectively

They are observations, and each is decided while the test is running. A witness
who arrives after it has begun should score them **not observed** and say so,
rather than inferring them from the numbers.

**Inferring them from the numbers is the specific error this rubric exists to
prevent.** The record a candidate produces when they get these wrong is the same
record they produce when they get them right — that is what makes the element an
equipment-route element in the first place.

## A quiet, well-behaved result is not evidence of a good instrument

On a **repeatability** test, a very small standard deviation should be read as a
prompt to check what the witness recorded about whether the load was genuinely
lifted between readings. A load left in place produces the smallest scatter the
candidate will ever see.

On an **eccentricity** test, a set of very small and remarkably uniform
differences should be read as a prompt to check whether the zero devices were
switched off before a taring variant began.

In both cases: score the first two components on the observation, never on the
plausibility of the result.

## The arithmetic component is not scored by inspection

**Repeatability** — the divisor is the number of depositions minus one.
Reviewers should recompute the standard deviation from the recorded indications
before scoring; at three depositions the difference from the wrong divisor is
fifty per cent of the variance.

**Eccentricity** — each off-centre indication must be differenced against the
centre indication that belongs to it, which on an interleaved method means its
own re-taken reading rather than one shared value. Reconstruct one difference by
hand. A table built on the wrong pairing is the right shape, the right size, and
wrong.

## Level differentiation

Set by `stated_procedure` and `confounder` in the binding, not by this rubric.

- **L1** — everything is stated and supplied. Execution and recording are what
  is observed, and a candidate who manipulates the load properly, settles, and
  records accurately scores well even though they decided nothing. The fifth
  component is close to empty here by design.
- **L2** — a procedure is supplied and the candidate performs the parts a
  procedure cannot perform for them, then reduces the data themselves. The
  second and fourth components carry the item.
- **L3** — nothing is stated. The candidate reads the instrument, selects
  against what they find, and records why. **The recorded justification is
  assessable evidence at this level**, and a correct selection made for a reason
  the candidate cannot state scores only partially on the fifth component.

## Score the reasoning, not the first choice

A candidate who selects a procedure, observes during the test that it was the
wrong one for this instrument, stops and restarts has demonstrated the
competence **more** convincingly than one who guessed correctly at the outset.
That applies to every test in the family and reviewers should apply it without
treating the restart as a fault.

Conversely, a candidate whose recorded justification is correct but who was
observed not to have looked at the instrument before writing it has produced the
right words about a decision they did not make. The fifth component is scored on
the decision, and the witness is the only one who can tell the difference.

## Two things that are not thoroughness

Running an eccentricity test in every range of a multiple-range instrument. It
answers the same mechanical question repeatedly and spends bench time the rest
of the calibration needs.

Taking more depositions than the instrument and load call for on a repeatability
test is not penalised — but taking **fewer** because the load is heavy, when it
sits below the threshold where cg-18 permits it, is. Note it under the fifth
component and ask the candidate what it does to the estimate.
