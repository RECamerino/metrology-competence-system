# Rubric — ARC-0005, Witnessed eccentricity test on a weighing instrument

Rubric-scored throughout, by a witness present for the whole test. **The
majority of the weight is on what the witness saw, not on what the candidate
handed over**, and that is the point of the archetype rather than an accident
of it.

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Zero devices handled correctly for the method used | 25% |
| Load placed by its centre of gravity, at the right positions | 20% |
| Indications recorded against the position they belong to | 15% |
| Differences computed with the pairing the method requires | 25% |
| Decisions the record would not otherwise show, stated | 15% |

## The witness has to be present before the test starts

The first component **cannot be scored retrospectively**. Whether zero-setting
and zero-tracking were switched off is decided in the seconds before the first
taring, and afterwards the instrument looks the same either way — a candidate
who left them on produces a small, uniform, wholly plausible set of
differences.

A witness who arrives after the test has begun should score this component as
not observed and say so, rather than inferring it from the numbers. **Inferring
it from the numbers is the specific error this rubric exists to prevent**: the
numbers are exactly what a candidate who got it wrong also produces.

## Small, uniform differences are not evidence of a good instrument

Where `stated_method` is one of the taring variants, a result showing very
small and remarkably even differences across all four positions should be read
as a prompt to check what the witness recorded about the zero devices — not as
a well-behaved instrument. Score the first component on the observation.

## Pairing is scored on the arithmetic, not on the shape of the table

The fourth component asks whether each off-centre indication was differenced
against the centre indication that belongs to it. On an interleaved method that
means its own re-taken centre reading, not a single shared one.

A table built on the wrong pairing is the right shape, the right size, and
wrong. Reviewers should reconstruct one difference by hand before scoring this
component; it is not visible by inspection.

## Level differentiation

Set by `stated_method` and `drift_present` in the binding, not by this rubric.

- **L1** — the method is stated, the load is supplied and the positions are
  marked. Execution and recording are what is observed. A candidate who places
  carefully, settles, and records against the right position scores well even
  though they decided nothing.
- **L2** — the method is stated and is one of the taring variants, so the zero
  devices carry real weight, and the candidate computes the differences
  themselves. The fifth component is light here: there is little to decide.
- **L3** — the method is not stated. The candidate has to notice how the
  instrument behaves and choose accordingly, adapt the positions to the
  receptor in front of them, and say whether the test load has to be a
  calibrated weight. **The recorded justification is assessable evidence at this
  level**, and a correct method chosen for a reason the candidate cannot state
  scores partially on the fifth component.

## What a failed attempt looks like, and what it does not

Choosing a non-interleaved method on an instrument that turns out to drift is
**not automatically a failure at L3**. A candidate who chooses method 1,
observes the drift during the test, stops, and restarts on an interleaved
method has demonstrated the competence more convincingly than one who guessed
correctly at the outset. Score the reasoning, not the first choice.

A candidate who runs the test in every range of a multiple-range instrument has
not been thorough. Note it under the fifth component and say why: it answers
the same mechanical question repeatedly and spends bench time the rest of the
calibration needs.
