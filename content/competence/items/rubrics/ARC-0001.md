# Rubric — ARC-0001, Standard uncertainty from an ambiguous source statement

Hybrid. The numeric part is auto-scored; everything below is applied by a
credentialed reviewer to the justification.

**The numeric part is worth little on purpose.** An AI assistant produces it
instantly, so it demonstrates almost nothing. It is retained because a
candidate who cannot do the arithmetic has not attained the element either.

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Correct standard uncertainty for the distribution the candidate assigned, units included | 20% |
| Distribution and divisor stated explicitly | 15% |
| Justification drawn from the wording of the statement | 45% |
| The most plausible alternative named and dismissed on stated grounds | 20% |

## Scoring the justification

Score against what the candidate did with the *wording*, not against whether
they reached the assignment the author had in mind.

**Full credit** — quotes or paraphrases the specific phrase they relied on,
says what it implies about the underlying population, and connects that to
the divisor chosen. Recognises where the source kind changes the reading: an
identical phrase from a calibration certificate and from a datasheet does not
carry identical weight.

**Partial credit** — assigns a defensible distribution and states a reason,
but the reason is generic ("manufacturer specifications are usually treated
as rectangular") rather than grounded in this statement.

**No credit** — applies a divisor with no engagement with the wording, however
correct the arithmetic. This is the failure mode the archetype exists to
catch, and it is common.

## Level differentiation

Difficulty comes from the draw, not from a different rubric.

- **L2** — statements whose reading is relatively clear; the candidate must
  identify it and apply it correctly.
- **L3** — statements where two readings are defensible; the candidate must
  pick one and say why.
- **L4** — the operating point interacts with the statement (percentage of
  reading against absolute, or a limit that does not apply across the whole
  range). A candidate who ignores that interaction cannot score above partial.

## Notes for reviewers

An answer reaching a *different* assignment from the one you would have made
is not thereby wrong. If the reasoning is sound and engages the wording, score
it full. Where two candidates justify opposite assignments equally well, both
are right — and if that happens often on one draw, the draw is ambiguous
beyond what the element intends, which is a defect to report rather than a
result to mark down.
