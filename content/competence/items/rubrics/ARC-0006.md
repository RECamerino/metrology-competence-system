# Rubric — ARC-0006, Witnessed verification of an indicating instrument

Rubric-scored throughout, by a witness present for the whole verification.
**The majority of the weight is on what the witness saw**, for the same reason
as `ARC-0005`: the record a candidate hands over is the same record whether or
not the work was done correctly.

The components below apply to every function in the family. What the witness is
looking for under each is stated on the **binding**, because it is a property of
the function being verified.

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| The connection was built as the method requires, and shown to be adequate | 30% |
| Each indication was allowed to settle before it was taken | 15% |
| The applied value was confirmed at the source, not assumed from the setting | 15% |
| Points recorded with the deviation and its sign, against the permitted error | 25% |
| Decisions the record would not otherwise show, stated | 15% |

## The connection carries the heaviest weight, and only a witness can score it

On the low ranges of most functions the connection is comparable in magnitude to
what is being judged. A verification performed on a poor connection produces a
complete table of plausible deviations that describe the leads rather than the
instrument.

The check that would have revealed it — a shorted-input or zero reading — has to
be **taken at the time**. A figure entered afterwards is indistinguishable in
the record from one observed, which is why this component is scored on the
observation and marked **not observed** if the witness arrived late. Do not
infer it from how good the numbers look.

## Settling and the applied value are separate failures

A candidate who reads immediately records a transient as a deviation. A
candidate who records the nominal value they set the source to, without
confirming what the source actually produced, has recorded an intention.

Both produce well-formed tables. They are scored separately because they are
different habits and a candidate often has one and not the other.

## The sign is not a formatting detail

The fourth component includes whether the deviation carries its sign. A record
of magnitudes discards whether the instrument reads high or low, which is the
half of the result that tells a user what will happen to their measurements.
Score it as wrong rather than untidy.

## Level differentiation

Set by `stated_procedure`, `confounder` and `standard_adequacy` in the binding.

- **L1** — the connection exists and the points are given. Execution and correct
  sign convention are what is observed. The fifth component is nearly empty by
  design.
- **L2** — the candidate builds the connection from a supplied procedure and
  works a specified list, including polarity coverage and the zero check. The
  first component becomes live here and stays the heaviest for the rest.
- **L3** — no list. The points come from the instrument's specification and the
  laboratory's scope, and the recorded justification becomes an assessable
  artifact alongside the data.
- **L4** — the procedure does not reach the case. Either the available standard
  cannot verify the specification at an adequate ratio somewhere, or the
  instrument's indication will not hold still. See below.

## What a good L4 answer looks like, and what it is not

**A restricted claim is a pass.** Where the standard cannot achieve an adequate
ratio at a point, saying so, reporting what was actually established, and
stating what would be needed to verify the specification is the correct answer.
Reviewers should score it as such rather than treating an incomplete
verification as an incomplete item.

**Reporting a clean pass the numbers do not support is the failure**, and it is
the one the archetype is built around. A candidate who verifies at a ratio too
poor to discriminate, and reports the result as though it discriminated, has
produced a certificate a laboratory will rely on.

Where the confounder is a drifting indication, a candidate who records several
readings and characterizes the drift has answered better than one who picks a
representative value — and much better than one who waits for a number they
like.
