# Rubric — ARC-0003, Defend a choice against a stated alternative

Rubric-scored. Double-scored at both levels the archetype serves.

**There is no correct position.** Defending and conceding score identically.
A reviewer who marks toward their own view has broken the item, and this is
the single most likely way this archetype fails in practice.

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Engages the strongest form of the opposing argument | 35% |
| Position stated and committed to | 20% |
| Conditions under which the candidate would change position | 25% |
| What evidence would settle the question | 20% |

## What scores badly

**The fluent survey.** An even-handed account of both positions that never
commits is the characteristic AI-assisted answer, and it scores near zero on
three of four components. This is not a penalty for using an assistant — it is
the item working as designed.

**Straw-manning.** Restating the counter-argument in a weaker form than the
prompt gave it caps the first component at partial regardless of what follows.

**Unfalsifiable commitment.** A position held with no stated conditions for
revising it scores zero on the third component. Certainty is not the same as
judgement, and at L5 the difference is the whole point.

## Level differentiation

- **L4** — the disagreement has a settled resolution in the literature the
  candidate is expected to know, even though both positions are defensible in
  practice.
- **L5** — the disagreement is genuinely open in the field. Reviewers must be
  correspondingly careful: at this level a candidate may hold a position the
  reviewer believes is wrong and still be scored full marks.

## Notes for reviewers

If you find yourself unable to score a response fairly because you hold a
strong position on the question, recuse yourself and say why. The reviewer
service record treats a recusal as a credit to your judgement, not a failure
to complete a review.
