  What the number an instrument is sold by actually buys. A digit or count
  figure fixes the smallest increment the display can show at each range, and
  that is the whole of what follows from it — a 6½-digit handheld can be less
  accurate than a 4½-digit reference meter and frequently is. The trailing

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0005`
§s02 — do not restate it here.

**This is a `knowledge` element.** The arithmetic that turns a range and a
count into an increment is trivial and is not what is being assessed; what is
assessed is what the candidate can say the number does and does not mean.
Computing a permitted error from the accuracy specification is `DP-08-064`.

**The L2 anchor asks for the nuance, not just the warning.** "More digits is
not more accurate" is the headline and a candidate can reach it by rote. The
part that separates L2 from L1 is being able to say what the trailing digits
*are* good for — resolving a change or a scatter well below the accuracy
figure, which is why anybody buys them. A candidate who concludes that the
extra digits are meaningless has overcorrected and has the wrong model.

**Where candidates fail.** Quoting the resolution as though it were the
uncertainty, which is the same defect as dividing a stated limit by the wrong
number one layer up. Computing resolution from full scale on the wrong range —
the increment differs per range and the digit figure is constant. Assuming a
half-digit doubles the count rather than roughly doubling the span of the
leading digit. And treating counts and digits as different specifications
rather than two ways of writing the same one.

**Ceiling 2 and every role at 2**, for the reasons set out in `DP-08-094` — no
role with DP-08 in scope needs less than the ceiling, and no role could never
need it.
