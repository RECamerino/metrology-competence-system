summary: >-
  Converting a calibration and measurement capability into the uncertainty that
  belongs on a particular certificate. The contributions evaluated for the best
  existing device are replaced by those of the device that arrived, so the
  reported figure normally exceeds the CMC — while the CMC simultaneously acts
  as a floor beneath which nothing may be reported. The two constraints point
  in opposite directions and are met at the same moment. What is assessed is a
  defended decision about which contributions were replaced, which carried
  over, and what the records show.
levelCeiling: 5
anchors:
  1: >-
    Given a scope entry and a certificate issued against it for a device of
    stated quality, decides whether the reported uncertainty could be correct,
    and says which direction it should differ from the CMC rather than
    treating the two figures as interchangeable.
  2: >-
    Given a CMC budget line by line and a specification for the customer's
    device, identifies which lines were evaluated for the best existing device
    and must be replaced, and which carry over unchanged, without adding the
    customer device's contributions alongside the ones they replace.
  3: >-
    For a calibration they have not met before, works from the records alone:
    performs the substitution, produces the reported uncertainty, checks it
    against the CMC floor, and records which contributions were replaced and
    on what evidence, so a reviewer can follow the derivation without
    reconstructing it.
  4: >-
    Decides a case where the customer device's contribution cannot be cleanly
    separated from the calibration process — or where the substituted result
    falls below the CMC and the cause is genuinely ambiguous between a lost
    contribution and an out-of-date scope entry — defends the treatment
    against a reviewer probing whether it was chosen to reach a wanted figure,
    and states what in the records would change it.
  5: >-
    Resolves the case where no best existing device exists for the measurand,
    or where the device contributions cannot be represented by any single
    value across the accredited range: reaches a position on whether the scope
    entry's CMC is demonstrably realisable as written, concedes plainly what
    the chosen treatment costs and in which direction, specifies what the
    scope must state for a reader comparing laboratories not to be misled, and
    brings another practitioner to the point of making the same call unaided.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0011` —
do not restate it here.

**This is a `judgment` element and the anchors ask for defended decisions,
because the arithmetic is trivial and the selection is not.** Replacing one
line of a budget with another and recombining is arithmetic an AI performs
instantly, and assessment is open-resource by design. What is assessed is which
contributions were held to belong to the device rather than the process, on
what evidence, and whether the candidate can defend that division to a reviewer
who reads it differently. A candidate who produces a correct number with no
account of what was replaced has not demonstrated the competence.

**The levels move on what the candidate is given, and on whether the case has a
clean answer.** At L1 the CMC and the certificate both exist and the question is
only which way they should differ. At L2 the budget is supplied line by line and
the device has a specification, so the substitution is identifiable by reading.
At L3 there are only records, and which contributions belong to the device
becomes the candidate's to establish. At L4 the separation is not clean, or the
floor and the substitution disagree. At L5 the premise itself fails — there is
no best existing device, or no single value represents the range — and the
question becomes whether the published capability is realisable at all.

**Where candidates fail, in order of frequency.** Reporting the CMC verbatim,
which satisfies the floor and skips the substitution entirely, and which is the
defect this element exists for. Adding the customer device's contributions
alongside the best existing device's rather than in place of them, producing a
figure that double counts the device — the mirror defect, treated in `BOK-0004`.
Treating a below-CMC result as a good outcome rather than as evidence that a
contribution has been lost. Including transport or handling contributions the
laboratory cannot know, or excluding them silently when they are expected to
dominate. And carrying a CMC that excludes device contributions under the
exceptional case without the scope entry saying so, which makes the laboratory
look better than a competitor while describing less.

**Boundary with `CM-03-083` and `CM-03-084`.** `CM-03-083` derives the CMC and
stops at the scope entry; this element starts there and ends at the certificate.
`CM-03-084` is uncertainty across a whole scope of accreditation, which is the
same operation viewed per scope rather than per job. The three were authored as
a chain and the middle link was missing — a person could hold `083` at L5 and
`084` at L4 and still report the CMC on every certificate they issued.

**The ceiling is 5 and the L5 anchor is not padding.** The no-best-existing-
device case is explicitly contemplated by the policy, it is rare, and getting it
wrong produces a scope entry that misleads every reader comparing laboratories.
An item at L5 that a competent practitioner could genuinely fail is writable:
give a measurand with no stable commercial artefact and a range over which the
device contribution varies by more than the process contribution, and ask
whether a single CMC figure can be published at all. Whether the candidate
reaches for the exclusion route, and whether they state the scope-marking
condition that comes with it, discriminates.

**What this element does not cover, deliberately.** Whether the reported
uncertainty is fit for the customer's conformity decision is `CM-08`, and the
guardband computed from it is `CM-08`'s to defend. This element ends when the
figure is correct for the device; what anyone does with it afterwards is a
different competence with a different failure mode.
