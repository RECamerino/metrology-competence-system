summary: >-
  Producing the combined standard uncertainty for a measurement: deriving a
  standard uncertainty for each contribution, assigning sensitivity
  coefficients, combining them under the law of propagation, and reporting the
  result at a stated coverage factor. This is the arithmetic centre of every
  budget and the point at which selection errors made earlier become a single
  number somebody else will rely on. What separates the levels is not the
  calculation — which is lookupable and always has been — but how much of the
  budget the candidate had to build before there was anything to combine.
levelCeiling: 4
anchors:
  1: >-
    Given a stated measurement model and every standard uncertainty and
    sensitivity coefficient supplied in a laid-out table, computes the combined
    standard uncertainty and the expanded uncertainty at a stated coverage
    factor, carrying units correctly at every line.
  2: >-
    Assembles the budget table themselves from supplied values, combines the
    contributions, and states what the reported figure means at the stated
    coverage factor — including that it is a standard uncertainty scaled by k
    and not a bound on the error.
  3: >-
    Works from raw records for a measurement they have not met before: derives
    each standard uncertainty from the calibration certificates and
    observations, assigns the sensitivity coefficients, combines them, and
    records which contributions were included and which were judged negligible
    so that a reviewer can follow the selection without reconstructing it.
  4: >-
    Handles the budget in which a contribution that would ordinarily be dropped
    as negligible is significant for the target in question: retains it, shows
    what it does to the combined figure, and defends the inclusion against a
    reviewer probing whether the selection was made to reach a wanted result,
    including saying what would change the decision.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0001`
§s03 — do not restate it here.

**This is a `skill` element and the anchors are performance, because the
calculation is not the competence.** Root-sum-square is lookupable, an AI
produces it instantly, and assessment is open-resource by design. What is
assessed is a budget the candidate produced: which contributions they included,
what they scaled them by, and whether the figure they reported is the one they
say it is. An item that can be passed by arithmetic alone is testing the wrong
thing and the binding should be corrected rather than the anchor loosened.

**The levels move on what the candidate had to build, not on difficulty of
sum.** At L1 the table exists and the values are in it. At L2 they lay the
table out. At L3 there is no table and no values — only certificates and
observations — and the selection becomes theirs, which is why recording the
basis first appears there. At L4 the selection is the whole question.

**Where candidates fail, in order of frequency.** Reporting an expanded
uncertainty labelled as a standard uncertainty, or the reverse; the numbers are
right and the claim is wrong, and a reader downstream compares it against
something incompatible. Dropping the sensitivity coefficient where the input
and the output happen to share units, which is correct by accident until the
model changes. Discarding a contribution as negligible against the largest term
rather than against the target, which is the L4 case exactly. Carrying a
correction and its uncertainty inconsistently, so the budget is defensible and
the result is not.

**The L3/L4 boundary.** L3 is a budget built unaided where the ordinary rules
of thumb hold: a contribution below roughly a fifth of the dominant term
contributes little to the root-sum-square and dropping it is defensible. L4 is
where that rule of thumb gives the wrong answer — a tight target, or several
comparable contributions, so a term that looks negligible is not. If an item at
L4 can be answered by applying the rule of thumb carefully, it is an L3 item.

**The ceiling is 4, and that is a claim worth testing.** An element whose L5
would be "combines uncertainties expertly" has no expert practice in it; the
genuinely expert work in this area lives in `CM-03-053` (correlation),
`CM-03-056` (higher-order terms) and `CM-03-040` (choosing distributions), each
of which is its own element with its own ceiling. If someone proposes raising
this one, the test in `docs/anchor-template.md` applies: bind an L5 item a
competent practitioner could genuinely fail, or leave the ceiling alone.

**Knowledge coverage was partial and is now most of the way closed, but this
element still cannot go `stable`.** Three gaps were recorded here originally.
`BOK-0002` §s04 closed the first — deriving a standard uncertainty from a
certificate's expanded uncertainty — and `BOK-0004` §s07 closed the third, the
negligibility judgement the L4 anchor turns on, together with `BOK-0003` §s05
on why the threshold is the stated target rather than a fixed fraction of the
dominant term. Both are now in `knowledgeRefs` above.

What remains is the second: **what a coverage factor means in the reporting
direction.** `BOK-0002` §s04 explains k well enough to *undo* it on somebody
else's certificate, which is what `CM-03-038` needs. It does not explain
choosing and stating one for a result this laboratory is reporting, and the L2
anchor here asks a candidate to say what the reported figure means at the
stated coverage factor. That subject belongs to `CM-03-060` and `CM-03-062`,
neither of which has an article yet.

So the refresher path is no longer broken for a holder who has forgotten how to
read a certificate; it is still broken for one who has forgotten what they are
asserting when they publish k equals two. Promotion out of `draft` waits on
that article — see rule 7: L3 and above cannot be attested against a draft
element, and three of this element's four bindings are at or above L3.

**Note for open decision 22.** This element is the worked example behind that
decision, and closing two of its three gaps did not change the underlying
problem: CI verified that the two new `knowledgeRefs` resolve, and could not
have told anyone whether they cover. That judgement was made by a human reading
both files, and it is recorded here because there is still nowhere in the data
to put it.
