summary: >-
  The evaluation method assumes every recognised significant systematic effect
  has been corrected, and sometimes a laboratory does not correct. This element
  is the decision and its consequences: distinguishing a genuinely negligible
  correction from a significant one left unapplied, recognising that enlarging
  the uncertainty to absorb a known displacement produces something that is not
  an uncertainty, and constructing a treatment that survives a downstream user
  combining the result with their own contributions. What is assessed is a
  defended position on which situation the laboratory is actually in.
levelCeiling: 5
anchors:
  1: >-
    Given a budget in which a correction is recorded as negligible, together
    with the correction's value and the combined standard uncertainty, decides
    whether the negligibility claim holds, and states what it was judged
    relative to rather than treating negligible as a property of the correction
    on its own.
  2: >-
    Given a reported result where a known correction was not applied and the
    stated uncertainty was enlarged instead, identifies that this has happened
    from the records, states what the reported interval does not carry that an
    uncertainty would, and names what the record should have contained for a
    reader to recover the decision.
  3: >-
    For a calibration curve they have not met before, works from the records
    alone: establishes whether the conditions for a single quoted uncertainty
    across a range genuinely hold, computes a mean correction and the separate
    variance contributions that go with it, produces the corrected estimate and
    its combined standard uncertainty, and records the decision so a reviewer
    can follow it without reconstructing it.
  4: >-
    Decides a case where the conditions only partly hold — the correction varies
    across the range but per-result computation is in fact feasible, or the
    quoted range extends beyond where the correction function was determined —
    defends applying or withholding the correction against a reviewer probing
    whether the choice was made to avoid work or to reach a wanted interval, and
    states what in the records would change the decision.
  5: >-
    Resolves a case where no treatment is clean and a result has already been
    issued and propagated: reaches a defensible position on what to do about the
    results already in circulation as well as the method going forward, concedes
    plainly what the chosen treatment costs and in which direction, specifies
    what the report and the record must carry for a downstream user to undo the
    decision for their own purpose, and brings another practitioner to the point
    of making the same call unaided.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0012` —
do not restate it here.

**This is a `judgment` element because the arithmetic is the small part.**
Computing a mean correction and combining three variance terms is bookwork an
AI performs instantly from the records. What is assessed is which of two
situations the laboratory is actually in — a correction genuinely negligible
against this measurement's combined uncertainty, or a significant one being
absorbed — and whether the candidate can defend that reading to a reviewer who
sees it the other way. The two look identical in a finished budget, which is
what makes the distinction worth assessing at all.

**The levels move on what the candidate is given and on whether the case has a
clean answer.** At L1 both numbers are supplied and the question is only what
negligibility was judged against. At L2 the decision has already been taken
badly and must be recognised from the records. At L3 there are only records, and
whether the conditions hold becomes the candidate's to establish. At L4 the
conditions hold in part, which is the common real case and the one with no
lookup. At L5 a result has already been issued and propagated, so the question
extends past method to what is owed to work already in circulation.

**Where candidates fail, in order of frequency.** Treating *negligible* as a
property of the correction rather than a comparison against this measurement's
combined standard uncertainty — the same correction is legitimately dropped for
a working instrument and required for a reference standard. Accepting a widened
interval as an uncertainty because it is conservative, without noticing it
carries no coverage probability and cannot be propagated. Applying the
mean-correction treatment while omitting the third variance term, which is the
one that exists only because of the decision and is therefore the one a
candidate who has pattern-matched the formula will drop. Extending a correction
function beyond the range over which it was determined. And recording the
resulting number without recording the decision, which leaves the next reader
unable to tell a considered treatment from an oversight.

**Boundary with `CM-03-016` and `CM-03-115`.** `CM-03-016` is representing a
correction and its uncertainty inside the model, and `CM-03-115` is applying
one — both assume the correction is applied, which is the assumption this
element suspends. It is the deliberate complement of the pair and was authored
because the corpus covered both halves of the default and nothing at all of the
departure from it.

**Boundary with `CM-03-102`.** The Guide's worked case is a calibration curve,
so the two elements meet there. `CM-03-102` is the uncertainty of interpolating
on the curve — a question about the curve. This element is the decision not to
use what the curve tells you, which is a question about the reporting policy the
curve is serving. An item that can be answered without reference to whether a
correction was applied belongs to `CM-03-102`.

**The ceiling is 5 and the L5 anchor is writable.** Give a candidate a
calibration curve issued for two years under the added-maxima convention, a
downstream customer who has been combining the interval with their own budget,
and a newly computed mean correction showing the displacement was larger than
assumed. Ask what is owed to the results already in circulation. A competent
practitioner can genuinely fail that, and it cannot be looked up — the Guide
supplies the treatment going forward and says nothing about remediation.

**One honest limitation, recorded here where a human will read it.** The
contested section in `BOK-0012` §s07 records a disagreement the Guide does not
resolve: it describes the added-maxima practice in one place and warns against
the general form of it in another. An item at L4 or L5 must not be scored on
which side the candidate takes. What is scoreable is whether they recognise the
tension exists, state which reading they are working under, and follow it
consistently — a candidate who picks the other position and defends it from the
records has demonstrated the competence.
