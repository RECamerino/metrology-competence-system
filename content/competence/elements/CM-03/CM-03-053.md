summary: >-
  Constructing and defending an uncertainty budget in which input quantities are
  not independent. Covers identifying the correlated pair, obtaining or bounding
  a correlation coefficient, carrying the covariance term through the
  propagation, and stating the basis on which the treatment rests. Ignored
  correlation is among the most common defects in real laboratory budgets, and
  it is not a conservative simplification — it under-reports whenever the
  sensitivity coefficients share a sign.
levelCeiling: 5
anchors:
  1: >-
    Given a budget in which two inputs are traceable to the same reference
    standard, and having been told what to look for, flags the pair as
    correlated rather than carrying them as independent lines.
  2: >-
    Adds a covariance term to a supplied budget for a pair they have identified,
    using a correlation coefficient they have been given, and shows what it does
    to the combined standard uncertainty and in which direction.
  3: >-
    Constructs a budget containing a covariance term for a measurement they have
    not worked on before: identifies the correlated inputs without being
    prompted, selects a correlation coefficient, records the basis for that
    selection, and lays the pair out so a reviewer can follow the relationship
    without reconstructing it.
  4: >-
    Handles the case the template does not cover — partial correlation, or
    opposite-signed sensitivity coefficients where the covariance term reduces
    the combined uncertainty rather than raising it — and defends the treatment
    against a reviewer probing it for weaknesses, including saying what would
    change the approach.
  5: >-
    Resolves a budget where the correlation structure cannot be estimated from
    data and a full bound would make the result unusable: produces a defensible
    treatment, states plainly what is assumed and what the assumption costs, and
    brings another practitioner to the point of doing the same work unaided.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject itself is explained in
`BOK-0001` — do not restate it here.

**This is a `skill` element, and the anchors are performance for that reason.**
"Understands why correlation matters" is a knowledge anchor and belongs to a
different element. What is assessed here is a budget the candidate produced.

**Where candidates fail, in order of frequency.** Treating the correlation as a
correction to apply rather than a structural feature of the model. Applying a
coefficient with no recorded basis, which is indistinguishable from an
arithmetic error to a reviewer. Assuming that ignoring correlation is
conservative — it is conservative only in the case that happens to help, which
is the case that matters less.

**The L4/L5 boundary.** L4 is the non-routine case handled well. L5 requires
that no established approach settles it: the coefficient cannot be estimated,
and bounding at full correlation inflates the result past usefulness. If an
item at L5 can be answered by applying a known method carefully, it is an L4
item and the binding should be corrected rather than the anchor loosened.
