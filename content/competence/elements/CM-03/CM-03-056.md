summary: >-
  Producing a budget for a model whose curvature the first-order propagation
  does not adequately represent: establishing that linearisation has stopped
  being adequate at the stated target, and carrying a treatment that is
  defensible given what is actually known about the inputs. The algebra of a
  higher-order term is lookupable. Noticing that the records call for one is
  not, because a first-order budget for a curved model is complete, arithmetically
  correct, and understated — and nothing in it says so.
levelCeiling: 5
anchors:
  1: >-
    Given a model, a first-order budget and a numerically propagated result for
    the same measurement side by side, produces the comparison between them and
    states whether the difference matters at the stated target uncertainty.
  2: >-
    Given a model with curvature already established and told to carry the
    higher-order term, produces the corrected combined standard uncertainty
    from supplied values and shows what the correction contributed relative to
    the first-order figure.
  3: >-
    Works from raw records for a measurement they have not met before,
    establishes that the first-order result is not adequate at the stated
    target, and produces a budget carrying the higher-order term. Nothing in
    the records flags the curvature, so identifying it is the performance and
    the algebra is not.
  4: >-
    Produces a budget for a model whose curvature sits near the threshold of
    mattering, where a first-order treatment and a higher-order one are both
    defensible: settles on one, records the reasoning against the stated
    target, and defends it against a reviewer arguing for the other — a
    first-order budget with the reasoning recorded standing as well as a
    corrected one.
  5: >-
    Produces a defensible treatment where the curvature is significant and the
    input distributions are not characterised well enough to justify either a
    higher-order term or a numerical propagation, so nothing in the records
    settles it: states plainly what the residual assumption is and what it
    costs, carries the limitation into what is reported rather than concealing
    it behind a corrected-looking figure, and brings another practitioner to
    the point of doing the same work unaided.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0003`
§s05 and §s06 — do not restate it here.

**This is a `skill` element and the anchors are performance, because the
higher-order expression is not the competence.** The formula is in the GUM and
an AI reproduces it on request. What is assessed is a budget the candidate
produced for a model whose curvature nothing in the records mentions: whether
they established that the first-order result was inadequate, against what
target, and what treatment they carried as a result.

**Not bound at L1 or L2, deliberately, and the binding file says why.** At
those levels the values and the model are supplied and the candidate is
applying a higher-order term they were told to apply, which observes none of
the judgement the element names. The coverage report will show both as gaps.
That is accurate and should not be closed by padding `ARC-0004` into the slots;
those levels want a different shape.

**The levels move on whether anything tells the candidate curvature is
present.** At L1 both results are laid side by side and the comparison is
read. At L2 the curvature is stated. At L3 nothing states it — the records are
ordinary and the first-order budget looks complete. At L4 the curvature is real
and near the threshold, so the decision rather than the detection is the work.
At L5 the detection and the decision are both done and neither treatment is
supported.

**L4 must not reward reaching for the correction.** A first-order budget with
the reasoning recorded scores as well as a higher-order one, because at the
threshold both are defensible and what is being observed is the recorded
judgement against the target. An item that scores the corrected answer higher
has turned the level into a test of whether the candidate remembered the
formula, which is L2.

**Where candidates fail, in order of frequency.** Never asking the question —
producing a first-order budget that is correct in every line for a model that
does not support one. Testing curvature against a fixed percentage instead of
against the stated target, so a discrepancy that is irrelevant to a comfortable
margin is corrected and one that decides a tight conformity call is not.
Carrying a higher-order term for a model whose inputs are known not to be
normally distributed, which is the assumption that correction is derived under
and the assumption a budget reaching for it has often already violated.
Treating a stable numerical coefficient as evidence that linearisation is
adequate — it is evidence about the derivative, not about the approximation the
derivative feeds. And reporting a corrected figure that looks authoritative
while resting on input distributions that were guessed, which is the specific
failure the L5 anchor is written against.

**The L5 case is where the element earns its ceiling.** Curvature significant,
input distributions insufficiently characterised, so the higher-order term
rests on an unsupported normality assumption and a numerical propagation rests
on distributions nobody has. No established approach settles it. The defensible
treatment is not a better calculation: it is the first-order result carried
with the limitation stated and visible on the certificate, so a reader can see
that the figure has a known weakness rather than a hidden one. If an item at L5
can be answered by choosing between the two established treatments, it is an L4
item and the binding should be corrected rather than the anchor loosened.

**Boundary with `CM-03-021`.** Judging *whether a model can be linearised at
all* is `CM-03-021`, is `judgment`, and sits in the modelling area. This
element takes the model as given and produces the budget, including the
decision about what to carry. The two are close and an item can test both by
accident; that is worth checking when a binding is reviewed.
