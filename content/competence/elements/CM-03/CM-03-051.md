summary: >-
  Obtaining a sensitivity coefficient by perturbing an input and observing the
  result, for a model that cannot be differentiated — a manufacturer's
  correction routine, a lookup table, a fitted surface, or the instrument
  itself with no written model at all. The ratio is trivial to compute and
  means nothing without the step size that produced it. Too large and it is a
  chord across a curve; too small and it is noise divided by a small number.
  Neither failure announces itself, and both return a plausible coefficient.
levelCeiling: 4
anchors:
  1: >-
    Given a model available only as a numerical routine, the inputs to perturb
    and the step size to use, produces each sensitivity coefficient by
    perturbing the input, recording the change in the result and taking the
    ratio, with the units carried through.
  2: >-
    Given a numerical model and a stated step size, produces the coefficients
    for a supplied budget, populates the table, and states what the estimate
    represents — a slope averaged across the perturbation, not the derivative
    at the point — and in what circumstances the two would differ.
  3: >-
    Works from raw records with no step size given: chooses the perturbation,
    produces the coefficients, and records the step used together with evidence
    that the estimate was stable across a range of steps, so a reviewer can see
    the choice was tested rather than assumed.
  4: >-
    Produces coefficients for a response that is unstable across every
    plausible step size, where no single perturbation is defensible on its own:
    demonstrates how far the estimate moves with the choice, carries the range
    the estimate spans rather than a single figure, and defends the treatment
    against a reviewer probing whether the step was selected for the answer it
    returned.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0003`
§s03 and §s04 — do not restate it here.

**This is a `skill` element and the anchors are performance, because the ratio
is not the competence.** Dividing a change in output by a change in input is
arithmetic. What is assessed is the coefficient set the candidate produced
*together with the evidence that the step was tested*, because a numerical
coefficient reported without its perturbation is a number with no method behind
it and cannot be checked by anybody.

**Not bound at L1, deliberately.** With every value supplied there is no
estimation left to observe. The coverage report will show L1 as a gap, and it
is a real one — the level wants a narrower shape than budget construction. The
anchor above exists because the level is attainable and needs a definition.

**The levels move on who chooses the step.** At L1 and L2 it is given, and what
is observed is the mechanics and then the candidate's account of what the
estimate represents. At L3 the choice is theirs and the *convergence evidence*
becomes the assessable artifact — an estimate stable across an order of
magnitude either way, shown rather than asserted. At L4 no plateau exists.

**The L4 case is a finding, not a failure, and items must reward it as one.** A
response unstable across every plausible step is telling the candidate that it
is not locally smooth at the scale that matters and that a single coefficient
will not represent it. The defensible answer is to carry the range and say so.
A candidate who picks a step, reports one number and moves on has produced a
well-formed budget line that is not supported by anything, and that is the
performance the L4 binding exists to separate from the competent one.

**Where candidates fail, in order of frequency.** Reporting a coefficient with
no step recorded, which is the defect this element exists to catch and which
appears at every level. Choosing a step far too small on the assumption that
smaller is closer to a derivative — it is, right up until the change in the
output is comparable to the routine's rounding, after which the estimate is
noise. Perturbing more than one input at a time. Failing to repeat where the
perturbation is applied to a physical instrument, so the estimate carries the
instrument's own noise with nothing recording how much. And treating a stable
estimate as a validated one: stability across steps says the derivative was
captured, and says nothing about whether the linear approximation it feeds is
adequate, which is `CM-03-056`.

**Boundary with `CM-03-050`.** That element is the model that can be
differentiated; this one is the model that cannot. The generator parameter
deciding which is what separates the two bindings, and without it both would be
budget construction wearing different labels.

**The ceiling is 4 and should stay there.** The genuinely expert work near this
element is deciding that no single coefficient represents the response at all —
which the L4 anchor already reaches at the level of one estimate, and which
becomes an L5 question only when it is the whole linearization in doubt. That
is `CM-03-056`.
