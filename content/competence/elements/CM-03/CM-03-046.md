summary: >-
  Deciding whether a Type B contribution covers an effect the Type A
  observations already captured, and defending the decision either way. One
  physical effect entered twice under two names overstates the uncertainty, and
  the resulting budget reads as thorough rather than defective — which is why
  it survives review. The question is never whether a specification mentions
  the effect; it is whether the repeats actually exercised it, and that turns
  on how the observations were taken rather than on what either document says.
levelCeiling: 5
anchors:
  1: >-
    Given a budget and told which two lines are suspected of covering the same
    effect, decides whether they do, and names the physical effect at issue
    rather than repeating the two labels the budget gave it.
  2: >-
    Given a budget together with a statement of how the repeated observations
    were taken, decides whether a carried specification covers an effect those
    observations already captured, and states the direction and rough size of
    the error if it does.
  3: >-
    For a budget they have not met before, works from the records alone:
    establishes what each set of observations actually varied, compares that
    against what each Type B source claims to cover, reaches a position on
    whether any effect is counted twice, and records the comparison so a
    reviewer can follow it without reconstructing it.
  4: >-
    Decides a case where one physical effect appears under two names drawn from
    documents written to different conventions, defends the removal — or the
    retention — against a reviewer who reads the two lines as distinct, and
    states what in the records would change the position.
  5: >-
    Resolves the partial overlap, where a specification covers some but not all
    of the variation the observations captured and neither removing the line
    nor keeping it in full is correct: produces a defensible treatment,
    concedes plainly what the residual assumption costs and in which direction,
    states the repeat programme that would remove the ambiguity for every
    future budget of that measurement, and brings another practitioner to the
    point of making the same call unaided.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0004`,
principally §s04 and §s05 — do not restate it here.

**This is a `judgment` element because the evidence is a decision defended, not
a calculation produced.** There is no arithmetic in it at all: removing a line
is a deletion. What is assessed is whether the candidate can establish that two
labels name one effect, and hold that position against a reviewer who reads the
documents differently.

**The competence is a comparison, and candidates skip half of it.** Two things
have to be established — what the observations varied, and what the Type B
source covers — and the second is easy because it is written down. The first is
not written down anywhere. It has to be reconstructed from how the repeats were
taken: back-to-back without disturbing the setup, or across setups, operators,
days, a temperature cycle. A candidate who reads only the specification will
reach a confident answer by comparing it against nothing.

**Where candidates fail, in order of frequency.** Deciding from the labels
rather than the effects, so "repeatability" and "short-term stability" are read
as two things because two documents used two words. Assuming that any set of
repeats captured the effect, which is false for the ten-readings-in-five-
minutes case that dominates real records. Removing the Type A contribution
rather than the specification where they coincide, which discards evidence
about this instrument in favour of a statement about a population. And treating
the overstatement as harmless — it is the reason the defect survives, and a
candidate who says so at L4 has answered a different question than the one
asked.

**The L4/L5 boundary.** L4 is a total overlap obscured by vocabulary: the
answer exists and finding it is the work. L5 is a *partial* overlap where the
fraction is exactly what the records do not say, so no calculation resolves it
and the field is divided on what to do. If an item at L5 can be settled by
establishing what the observations varied, it is an L4 item and the binding
should be corrected rather than the anchor loosened.

**Sibling of `CM-03-019`, and the pair is worth binding together eventually.**
That element is the missing contribution; this one is the duplicated one. They
are the two defects arithmetic cannot find, they run in opposite directions,
and a budget can carry both at once — which is the case neither element's
current bindings reach.
