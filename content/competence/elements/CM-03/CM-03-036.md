summary: >-
  Turning a source statement that gives bounds and nothing else into a standard
  uncertainty: recognising the wording that describes a limit, assigning the
  shape that adds no information of the author's own, applying the divisor, and
  recording the reading the line rests on. The arithmetic is one division. What
  is assessed is whether the candidate can tell a stated limit from a coverage
  interval, because dividing a limit by two instead of root three is the single
  commonest defect in Type B lines and the resulting budget looks perfect.
levelCeiling: 4
anchors:
  1: >-
    Given a source statement already identified as a stated limit and told the
    shape to assign, produces the standard uncertainty for that budget line —
    half-width divided by root three — with the divisor written out rather than
    folded into the result, and the units carried.
  2: >-
    Given a source statement with no shape indicated, assigns rectangular,
    produces the contribution, and records the wording relied on. The statement
    points clearly at a limit, and what is observed is that the candidate
    divides by root three rather than by two and uses the half-width rather
    than the full width.
  3: >-
    For a source statement they have not met before, where rectangular is
    defensible but not the only available reading — a limit the source also
    describes as typical performance — assigns rectangular, produces the
    contribution, and records both the reading taken and why the alternative
    was not, so a reviewer can disagree with the reading rather than only with
    the number.
  4: >-
    Produces the assignment for a source the rule of thumb does not fit:
    asymmetric bounds, a limit stated across a range wider than the operating
    point, or a limit that already encompasses an effect carried elsewhere in
    the budget. Shows what the naive assignment would have given, and defends
    the line against a reviewer probing whether the shape was chosen for the
    number it produces.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0002`
§s02 — do not restate it here.

**This is a `skill` element and the anchors are performance, because the
arithmetic is not the competence.** Dividing by root three is a single
keystroke and an AI produces it instantly. What is assessed is the budget line
the candidate produced: which reading of the source they took, what they used
as the half-width, and whether a reviewer can tell from the line itself that a
limit was recognised rather than a divisor guessed.

**The levels move on how much of the reading the candidate had to do.** At L1
the statement has already been classified and the shape named. At L2 they
classify it, from wording that points clearly at a limit. At L3 the wording
admits a second reading and the recorded justification becomes the assessable
part. At L4 the statement does not fit the shape of the rule at all.

**Where candidates fail, in order of frequency.** Dividing by two, which treats
a stated limit as a coverage interval and understates the line by about fifteen
per cent. Using the full width rather than the half-width, which overstates by
a factor of two and survives review longer than it should because the budget
looks cautious. Assigning rectangular with no recorded reason, which is
indistinguishable from a mistyped divisor to the only person in a position to
catch it. And selecting rectangular *because* it gives the larger number —
which is not a defence, and which an L4 item is built to expose.

**Boundary with `CM-03-040`.** This element is assigning rectangular and
defending that assignment. Choosing *between* candidate shapes where more than
one is genuinely defensible is `CM-03-040`, is `judgment` rather than `skill`,
and carries a ceiling of 5. If an item here cannot be answered without first
settling a contested selection, it belongs to that element instead.

**The ceiling is 4 and should stay there.** An L5 on this element would have to
describe expert practice in applying one divisor, and there is none. The expert
work in this area is the selection, and it has its own element.
