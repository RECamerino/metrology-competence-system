summary: >-
  Recovering a standard uncertainty from a source that has already done the
  evaluation and reported it at a coverage — dividing by a stated coverage
  factor, by the factor a stated confidence implies, or, where the certificate
  states neither, making the assumption explicitly instead of silently. This is
  the inverse of assigning from a limit, it is the operation performed on
  almost every calibration certificate a laboratory receives, and the case it
  exists for is the one where the certificate does not say what k was.

  4: >-

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0002`
§s04 — do not restate it here.

**This is the inverse operation of `CM-03-036` and the pair is deliberate.**
There, a limit is turned into a standard uncertainty by assigning a shape.
Here, a figure that has already been scaled is unscaled. A candidate who has
only ever met one of the two reliably applies the wrong divisor to the other,
and the two elements exist separately so that a budget can record which
operation was performed.

**The levels move on how much the certificate tells the candidate.** At L1 the
factor is stated and the work is a division. At L2 the confidence is stated and
a distributional assumption has to be supplied and named. At L3 the wording is
unfamiliar and deciding which route it supports is the work. At L4 the
certificate supports neither route, and what is assessed is whether the
candidate makes their assumption visible.

**Where candidates fail, in order of frequency.** Applying k equals two to a
certificate that never said so — usually correct, always unrecorded, and
therefore indistinguishable from a candidate who did not notice the factor was
missing. Reporting the recovered figure under the name of the one it came from,
so a standard uncertainty travels downstream labelled as expanded, or the
reverse; the arithmetic is right and the claim is wrong, and no check on the
arithmetic will find it. Treating a percentage specification as a coverage
statement because it is written with a plus-or-minus sign. And recovering from
a confidence level without recording that normality was assumed, which hides
the one judgement the line contains.

**The L4 case is the element's reason for existing.** A certificate stating an
expanded uncertainty with no coverage factor and no confidence level is common,
particularly from outside an accredited scope, and there is no correct answer
available from the document. The defensible treatment is explicit: state the
assumption, show what the alternative would give, and — where the difference
changes the outcome — say that the issuing laboratory should be asked rather
than choosing. An item at L4 that can be passed by dividing by two and moving
on is testing L1 and the binding should be corrected.

**Boundary with `CM-03-060`.** What a coverage factor *means*, and how one is
chosen for a result this laboratory is reporting, is `CM-03-060` and is
`knowledge`. This element is the recovery operation performed on somebody
else's reported figure.
