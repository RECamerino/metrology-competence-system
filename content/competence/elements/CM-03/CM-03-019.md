summary: >-
  Establishing whether an uncertainty budget accounts for everything that
  matters, when the defect leaves no trace in the table. Every line can be
  correct and the budget still be wrong, because nothing in it refers to what
  is absent — so a completeness check has to generate its candidates from the
  measurement rather than from the document under review. Reading a budget and
  asking whether anything seems missing produces the sources the reader happens
  to think of, which are largely the ones the author thought of.

  4: >-

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0004`,
principally §s01 and §s02 — do not restate it here.

**This is a `judgment` element and the anchors describe positions reached and
defended.** There is nothing to compute. The observable behaviour is a search
carried out and its result argued for, and — from L3 up — a written record of
what was examined and dismissed, which is the only part of a completeness check
that exists after the fact.

**Item design has one hard constraint: the defect must not be visible in the
table.** An item where the omission can be found by reading the budget is
testing budget review, not completeness. The bindings fix the injected class to
a missing influence quantity and choose contexts where the omission is
discoverable only by reasoning about the physical measurement — a thermal
gradient in a long gauge block comparison, buoyancy absent from a mass
comparison, thermal EMF unaccounted in a four-wire resistance measurement. Each
of those is a budget in which every present line is correct.

**Where candidates fail, in order of frequency.** Reviewing the budget instead
of the measurement, which finds arithmetic and nothing else. Generating
candidates from the budget's own vocabulary, so the search inherits the
author's blind spots. Producing a finding with no account of what else was
looked at, which leaves a reviewer unable to distinguish a search from a lucky
guess. And treating an absence as automatically a defect: at the stated target
a great many omissions are correct, and the L5 case is exactly the one where
dismissing the quantity was the right call.

**The L4/L5 boundary.** At L4 the missing quantity matters and the work is
finding it and holding the finding. At L5 it is one a competent laboratory
could reasonably have judged negligible, so the defensible answer may be that
the budget is sound as written provided the judgement was recorded — and the
candidate has to decide in either direction and defend that. An item at L5 with
an omission that clearly matters is an L4 item.

**The recorded-dismissal requirement first appears at L3, and that is
deliberate.** Below it the candidate is working against a supplied list, so
there is nothing about the search that is theirs. From L3 the list is theirs,
and the list is the evidence.

**Sibling of `CM-03-046`.** That element is one effect counted twice; this one
is an effect not counted at all. They are the two defects arithmetic cannot
find and they run in opposite directions.
