title: Adjustment of a balance, and what it does to as-found data

summary: >-
  Deciding whether to adjust a weighing instrument before calibrating it, and
  holding a defensible position on what that does to the record. The default is
  to adjust — a calibration characterizes the instrument in the state it is used
  in, and a routinely adjusted instrument is never in the unadjusted state
  during real work — but the same act destroys the only evidence of how the
  instrument was performing since its last calibration, permanently and
  silently. The guide names the agreement that displaces the default and says
  nothing about when to seek it. The general judgement is CM-06-027, a
  prerequisite; what is assessed here is what it costs on a weighing
  instrument.
levelCeiling: 4
anchors:
  1: >-
    Given a record of a weighing instrument's calibration and a statement of
    whether it was adjusted beforehand, says which state the as-found figures
    describe — the instrument as it arrived, or the instrument as it is used —
    and, told that the adjustment was carried out at one test load, says
    whether the indications recorded at the other test loads are affected by it
    and why. States one thing the record therefore cannot answer.
  2: >-
    For a routine weighing instrument, applies the default correctly: adjusts an
    instrument whose client adjusts it before every use, performs the adjustment
    by the means that client normally applies — their procedure and their
    weights, not the better ones the laboratory owns — and records that the
    adjustment was made, by what means, and at what load.
  3: >-
    For a client and an instrument they have not met before, decides whether the
    default applies by establishing how the instrument is actually used rather
    than assuming, and applies the scope distinction this pack's own tests
    create: the zero-setting and zero-tracking devices stand as the client sets
    them for the calibration as a whole, while the eccentricity test switches
    them off for the variants that would otherwise measure the device instead of
    the instrument. Records the decision, what it rests on and how the devices
    were set, and refers a conflict between the client's wish and the default
    for agreement before the instrument is touched.
  4: >-
    Handles the case where the two readings of as-found data pull in opposite
    directions: a client who has to evaluate the validity of weighings already
    performed, and an instrument whose in-service state is the adjusted one.
    Produces a defensible course, states plainly what each choice costs the
    client and which loss is irreversible, and defends the position against a
    reviewer arguing the other reading in its strongest form — including saying
    what would change the decision.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0017` —
do not restate it here.

**It is also the first `EC` spine element anyone wrote out, and it is the worked
example for the spine test because it failed that test first.** Rule 14 says the
spine — receiving inspection, configuration, standards and fixturing, then
adjustment, uncertainty, conformity — repeats because it genuinely does. That is
true of the work and does not make itself true of the file. The title named a
balance, the citations and the notes below were full of weighing-specific
material, and all four anchors said *instrument*: substitute a torque wrench or a
DMM and every one still read correctly, which is the project's own disqualifier
for an `EC` element. The competence they described was `CM-06-027`, and `CM-06`
appeared nowhere in this file. 204 elements share this spine step's title
template. The anchors now carry the two things the rest of the file already knew
and they did not — what an adjustment at one load does to the indications at the
others, and whose setting governs the zero devices — and `CM-06-027` is a
prerequisite so the general judgement is established once.

**This is the first `judgment` element in the `EC` axis, and it settles a
question the three tests before it had left open.** `EC-04-004`, `005` and `007`
are all `skill` and all `[equipment]`, and it would have been easy to conclude
that `EC` elements are witnessed bench work by nature. They are not. The axis is
organized by the equipment being calibrated; the kind decides how the competence
is evidenced, and this one is evidenced by explanation and defence. The element
schema says so in as many words — knowledge and judgment are desk work by
definition — so `demonstration: [desk]` here follows from `kind` and is not a
separate call.

**The consequence for the item bank is worth stating.** `ARC-0005`, the
witnessed archetype built for this area, **cannot serve this element and should
not be made to.** What is assessed is a decision, and watching somebody make a
decision tells you almost nothing that their defence of it does not tell you
better. The binding uses `ARC-0003` instead, which is the second cross-axis
reuse in the corpus.

**What makes it a judgement rather than a rule to follow.** The guide's default
is clear: adjust before calibrating, for instruments that are routinely adjusted
before use. It also names the agreement that displaces the default — *unless
otherwise agreed with the client* — and then says nothing whatever about when
that agreement should be sought. The gap is real, practice has filled it in two
directions, and both are defensible. `BOK-0017` §s06 records them as a contested
section with each position in its strongest form.

**The asymmetry a candidate has to see.** The two losses are not the same kind
of loss. Calibrating an instrument in a state it never occupies produces a
certificate of limited use, which is bad and is recoverable — the instrument can
be calibrated again. Adjusting before capturing as-found data destroys the only
evidence of how the instrument behaved during a period that has already
happened, and nothing recovers it. An L4 answer that treats the two as
symmetrical trade-offs has missed the feature that makes the decision urgent
rather than merely important.

**Where candidates fail.**

Treating the default as a rule and applying it without establishing how the
client uses the instrument. The default is conditional on the instrument being
*intended to be regularly adjusted before use*, and a candidate who does not
check has applied a conditional as though it were universal.

Adjusting with the laboratory's standards because they are better than the
client's. It produces a certificate describing an instrument in a condition it
reaches only when a calibration engineer is in the room.

Resolving the conflict at the bench when it should have been referred. The
agreement cg-18 names is an agreement *with the client*, and a laboratory that
decides on the client's behalf has made a choice about the client's evidence
without telling them.

Discovering the question after the adjustment. Every other failure here is
correctable and this one is not, which is why the L3 anchor asks for it to be
recognized before the instrument is touched.

Conflating the client's setting of the automatic zero devices with the
eccentricity test's requirement that they be off. The scopes differ, and a
candidate who overrides the first in the name of the second has changed what the
whole calibration describes.

**For item authors.** Bound at **L4 only**, and the reason is in the binding
file: `ARC-0003` declares levels 4 and 5, and the lower levels of this element
want a shape the bank does not yet have. The coverage report will show L1 to L3
as gaps and that is accurate rather than an oversight.
