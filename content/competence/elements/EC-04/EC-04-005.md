summary: >-
  Establishing whether a weighing instrument's indication depends on where the
  load sits on its receptor: placing a test load at the centre and at four
  off-centre positions, and evaluating the differences. The test whose result is
  most easily destroyed by the instrument itself — two of the four procedural
  variants require the automatic zero devices to be switched off, and leaving
  them on removes the very effect being measured while producing a record that
  looks entirely normal.

    determine errors of indication, and records those choices so that a reviewer
    can follow the selection without reconstructing it.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0013` —
do not restate it here.

**The levels move on how much of the method belongs to the candidate.** At L1
the method is stated, the load is supplied and the positions are marked, so what
is observed is placement accuracy, settling, and recording each indication
against the right position. At L2 the candidate runs a supplied procedure and
the assessable content is the two things a procedure cannot make happen by
itself — switching the zero devices off when the method requires it, and using
the correct pairing when computing the differences. At L3 there is no stated
method: the instrument's behaviour selects it, and the recorded justification
becomes an assessable artifact alongside the data.

**Ceiling 3, and it is not a placeholder.** The test is bounded. There is no
case here that the guideline does not reach, no legitimate professional
disagreement about how to run it, and nothing an expert does that a competent
technician does not — which is exactly what a ceiling of 3 is for. The
judgement that *does* exist at the top of this element is method selection
against observed drift, and that is an L3 judgement rather than an L4 one
because the guideline tells you the answer once you have noticed the drift.
Elements around it are ceilinged higher for real reasons: `EC-04-014`
(uncertainty budget) and `EC-04-013` (adjustment and what it does to as-found
data) both reach cases the method does not cover.

**Where candidates fail, in order of how invisible the failure is afterwards.**

Leaving zero-tracking on for a method that requires it off. This is first
because it cannot be recovered from the record: the device removes the
differences as they appear, and the result is a small, uniform, entirely
plausible set of numbers. A reviewer reading the certificate has nothing to go
on, and the instrument reads as well-behaved. **Only a witness sees this**, and
only if they are watching before the test starts.

Differencing against a shared centre indication when the method took one per
position. Method 4 is chosen *because* the instrument drifts; collapsing its
centre readings into one discards the interleaving and puts the drift straight
back into the result. The table that comes out is the right shape and the right
size and wrong.

Placing the load by its footprint rather than its centre of gravity, which
matters most with a large or awkward test load and least with a compact one —
so it is the candidate using the convenient weight who is most exposed.

Using an uncalibrated test load when the same indications are also being used
to determine errors of indication. The combination is attractive because it
saves a loading, and it is where the requirement is most often missed.

Running the test in every range of a multiple-range instrument, which is not
thoroughness. It answers the same mechanical question repeatedly and spends
bench time the rest of the calibration needs.

**A note for item authors, and it is the reason `ARC-0005` exists.** Every
failure above except the last is invisible in the record the candidate hands
over. This element cannot be assessed by reading a submitted artifact, because
the artifact is exactly what a candidate who did it wrongly also produces. It is
assessed by somebody watching, which is what `demonstration: [equipment]` means
here and why the archetype bound to it is a witnessed performance rather than a
prompt.
