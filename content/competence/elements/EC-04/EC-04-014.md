    the two enclosing standard deviations where several test loads were used —

    contributions were included and which were judged negligible so a reviewer
    can follow the selection.
  4: >-
    Handles the budget the template does not reach: a test load built from
    several standard weights, where the uncertainties are correlated and must be
    summed arithmetically rather than in quadrature, or an instrument whose
    repeatability varies across the range so that no single uncertainty can be
    quoted for the whole of it. Produces a defensible treatment, states what the
    assumption of independence would have cost and in which direction, and
    defends the result against a reviewer probing whether the contributions were
    selected to reach a wanted figure — including saying what would change it.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0016` —
do not restate it here.

**This is the first `demonstration: [desk]` element in the `EC` axis, and that
is worth stating plainly because the axis is easy to misread.** `EC` is
organized by the equipment being calibrated, not by where the work happens.
Three of this area's elements are bench work and this one is not: given the test
records and the weights' certificates, the budget is built at a table. Rule 11
is explicit that constructing an uncertainty budget is a skill and is desk work,
and marking this element `equipment` because its neighbours are would invent a
barrier for exactly the learner the Personal edition exists for — somebody who
can obtain calibration records far more easily than a balance.

**Where the boundary with `CM-03` actually falls.** `CM-03-081` is building a
budget for a *mass measurement*; this is the budget for the *calibration of the
instrument*, and its contributions are the instrument's own test results — the
repeatability standard deviation, the eccentricity differences, the scale
intervals at zero and at load. It cannot be written without naming the
equipment, which is rule 14's test, and it is the element the three
measurement-method tests exist upstream of.

**The levels move on how much of the budget is the candidate's**, and L4 is a
genuine case rather than a harder version of L3. At L1 everything is evaluated
and the work is combination. At L2 the contributions are identified and the
candidate evaluates them from supplied values. At L3 the records are raw and the
selections — which repeatability figure, whether eccentricity arises — are the
assessable content. At L4 the budget's central assumption fails.

**The L4 case is correlation, and it is not a trick.** A test load built from
several standard weights carries several uncertainties that are not independent:
the weights were calibrated against the same references, by the same laboratory,
often in the same session. cg-18 requires them to be summed arithmetically for
that reason. A candidate who combines them in quadrature has assumed
independence, and **the result is understated** — the error is not random and is
not conservative. An L4 candidate should be able to say that the rule is the
general correlated expression at full positive correlation rather than a local
convention, which is why `BOK-0001` is among the knowledge refs.

**Where candidates fail, in order of consequence.**

Adding the weights' uncertainties in quadrature. It is the single most
consequential error available here and it always moves the answer the same way.

Quoting one uncertainty for the whole weighing range without checking the two
conditions that permit it — a constant repeatability standard deviation and no
eccentricity contribution. Most laboratory templates quote one figure, so this
is inherited rather than decided.

Applying the divisor for a mean of N readings to a single reading, which
understates; or omitting it where the indication genuinely was a mean, which
overstates. The first is the dangerous direction and the record often does not
say which the indication was.

Interpolating between two enclosing repeatability standard deviations instead of
taking the greater. It looks more careful and is not what the guide asks for.

Carrying an eccentricity term for a calibration performed with a single-body
test load, which inflates the budget for an effect that did not arise.

Missing the type-approval case at the zero rounding term, which is worth a
factor of two and requires knowing something about the instrument that the test
data does not contain.

**For item authors.** Bound to `ARC-0004` — the budget-construction archetype
written for `CM-03`. This is the **first cross-axis binding in the corpus**, and
it is the test of whether a desk archetype spans axes the way `ARC-0005` spans a
family of procedures. `measurement_context` carries the equipment; the
`structural_feature` generator carries what makes the item about *this* element
rather than about budgets in general.
