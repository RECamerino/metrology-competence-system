summary: >-
  Determining what a weighing instrument reads against known masses across its
  range: selecting and distributing the test loads, applying them in a sequence
  that matches how the instrument is used, and differencing each indication
  against the reference value of the mass. The third of cg-18's measurement
  methods and the one the other two defer to — it is the test that turns a test
  load from a convenient mass into a reference value, and that cannot be decided
  after the loading.

    and computes the error at each point against the reference value supplied.
  2: >-

  3: >-

    across its range and the loading sequence from how the instrument is
    actually used, decides whether the nominal values of the weights are
    adequate as reference values or whether their conventional values are needed
    and says what that judgement rests on, handles the reduced-range or
    multiple-range case where the points do not sit where the standard pattern
    puts them, and records those choices so a reviewer can follow the selection.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0015` —
do not restate it here.

**This is the third of the three and it is not interchangeable with them.** A
laboratory performs repeatability, eccentricity and errors of indication in one
session on one bench, and `EC-04-004`, `EC-04-005` and this element are
deliberately separate because what a candidate has to get right differs in each.
Read all three together before authoring items for any of them.

**The provision that makes this element the keystone.** The other two tests say
the load need not be calibrated — *unless the results also serve to determine
errors of indication*. That clause points here. So this is the test that turns a
mass into a reference value, and the consequence is a sequencing trap rather
than a knowledge one: **a laboratory that runs all three on one loading has to
have used calibrated weights from the first reading**, and cannot decide
afterwards that the data will also serve this test. A candidate who understands
the requirement perfectly and discovers it at the wrong moment has still lost
the data.

**The levels move on how much of the method belongs to the candidate**, as on
the other two. At L1 the loads, the points and the sequence are given. At L2 the
candidate checks the loads against the pattern's deviation and spacing
conditions and states which reference values they used. At L3 nothing is stated
and the selection is the assessable artifact.

**Ceiling 3.** The guideline reaches every case this test contains, and the
judgements in it — the sequence, the reference values, the reduced-range pattern
— all have answers once the instrument and its use have been read correctly.
What sits above this is `EC-04-014`, where the errors become an uncertainty
budget, and that reaches cases the method does not cover.

**Where candidates fail, in order of how invisible it is afterwards.**

Applying the loads without unloading between steps when the stated sequence
calls for unloading. The record is a list of indications either way, and the
result quietly contains creep the instrument's users will never see.

Using nominal values as reference values on an instrument fine enough that the
weights' corrections matter — and not saying which was used, so a reader cannot
tell whether the choice was made or defaulted into. Summing several weights
makes this worse rather than better, because the corrections sum too.

Using the deviation allowance to bring two neighbouring test points close
together. The record shows the right number of points; the range has a gap in
it.

Taking the reduced-range concession without agreeing a reduced range, which is
a narrower claim being reported as a full one.

Discovering after the loading that the same indications are wanted for this test.
The weights were what they were.

**For item authors.** Bound to `ARC-0005`, which spans cg-18's measurement
methods — this is the third test in that family and the one the archetype was
widened to accommodate rather than the one it was written for. The binding
carries what a witness must see for *this* test; the archetype carries what all
three require.
