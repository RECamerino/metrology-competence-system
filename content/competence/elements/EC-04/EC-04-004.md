summary: >-
  Establishing the scatter of a weighing instrument's indication when the same
  load is deposited on it repeatedly under identical handling: sizing the load,
  choosing the number of depositions, holding the conditions constant, and
  reducing the indications to a standard deviation. The test whose result is
  easiest of all to make look good — reading the display several times without
  lifting the load produces a record indistinguishable from a correct one, and
  a scatter that belongs to the electronics rather than to weighing.
levelCeiling: 3
anchors:
  1: >-
    With the test load supplied, the test point stated and the number of
    depositions specified, lifts the load clear of the receptor and deposits it
    again for each reading, allows the indication to settle, records each
    indication, and checks the no-load indication after every removal.
  2: >-
    Works the test from a supplied procedure: confirms the test load is
    reasonable against the capacity and the scale interval of the instrument in
    front of them, applies the number of depositions that load calls for,
    records the no-load indications and the status of the zero-setting or
    zero-tracking device, and computes the experimental standard deviation with
    the correct divisor.
  3: >-
    For an instrument they have not calibrated before, selects the test load and
    the number of depositions from its capacity, its scale interval and its
    construction — including the multi-interval and multiple-range cases where
    the load belongs near the top of a partial range rather than near capacity —
    keeps the handling identical across depositions and states what they held
    constant to achieve it, says whether the load must be a calibrated weight
    because the same indications will also determine errors of indication, and
    records those choices so a reviewer can follow the selection.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0014` —
do not restate it here.

**Read this element beside `EC-04-005`.** They are the same instrument, the same
bench, the same test load provisions and the same hour of work, and a laboratory
performs them back to back. What separates them is what the witness has to
watch, and that difference is the reason both exist as elements rather than one.

**The failure this element is built around is the easiest in the corpus to
commit and the hardest to detect.** The test is the repeated *deposition* of a
load. A candidate who leaves the load on the pan and reads the display five
times produces five indications, a mean, and a standard deviation — a complete,
well-formed, entirely plausible record, with a scatter that is smaller than the
truth because it contains none of the mechanical variability the test exists to
capture. Nothing downstream can recover it. **The uncertainty budget the
laboratory then builds on that figure is understated for the life of the
instrument's calibration**, and the certificate is not wrong on its face.

**The levels move on how much of the method belongs to the candidate**, as they
do on `EC-04-005`. At L1 the load, the point and the count are all given, so
what is observed is whether the load is genuinely lifted each time and whether
the zero is checked after each removal. At L2 the candidate works a procedure
and is assessed on the two things a procedure cannot perform for them —
recording the zero device status, and using the right divisor. At L3 nothing is
stated and the selection itself is the assessable artifact.

**Ceiling 3, for the same reason as `EC-04-005`.** The test is bounded and the
guideline reaches every case it contains. The judgement that exists — sizing the
load on a multi-interval or multiple-range instrument, and deciding whether the
concession to three depositions applies — is an L3 judgement because the
guideline supplies the answer once the instrument has been read correctly.

**Where candidates fail, in order of how invisible it is afterwards.**

Not lifting the load between readings. First, and nothing in the record shows
it.

Varying the handling without noticing: placing centrally once and off-centre
after, setting the load down at different speeds, letting different intervals
pass before reading. Each adds its own component and every one of them is
reported as the instrument's.

Using a stack where a single body was available. The stack shifts between
depositions and is handled differently from a single mass, so it contributes
variability of its own.

Taking the three-deposition concession on a load below 100 kg. It is not a
choice between five and three; it is a materially poorer estimate of the
standard deviation, and the divisor makes the penalty larger than it looks.

Recording nothing about the zero-setting or zero-tracking device. Unlike the
eccentricity test, this one does not require the device to be off — it requires
its status to be **recorded**, and a candidate who has learned the other rule
and switched it off has done something defensible, while one who wrote nothing
has left the result ambiguous.

**For item authors.** This element and `EC-04-005` are both bound to `ARC-0005`,
which was widened from a single test to the family cg-18 groups under its
measurement methods. The archetype carries what every witnessed test on a
weighing instrument requires; the binding carries what a witness must see for
*this* test, because that is a property of the procedure and the binding is
where the procedure is chosen.
