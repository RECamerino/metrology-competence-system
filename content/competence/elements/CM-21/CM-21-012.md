summary: >-
  Deciding whether a reference in technical text can be relied on, when a
  fabricated one is formatted correctly, numbered plausibly and reads exactly
  like the real ones beside it. Four things can be wrong — document, edition,
  clause number, claim — and each check needs more access than the one before.
  The failure worth building a competence around is the last: a reference that
  resolves to a real clause which does not say what it was cited for, and which
  every check short of reading the clause will pass.
levelCeiling: 4
anchors:
  1: >-
    Given a short technical passage containing several clause references and
    told that one of them is wrong, decides which, and states what about it
    does not hold up — a clause number outside the document's range, a
    designation that does not name a real standard, an edition that was never
    issued.
  2: >-
    For a supplied passage, decides for each reference whether it can be
    confirmed from the material available, separates the ones that check out
    from the ones that cannot be checked without the document in hand, and
    states which claim in the passage each reference was doing work for.
  3: >-
    For technical text they have not seen before with no indication that
    anything is wrong, works out which claims rest on a reference, checks each
    against the source, reaches a position on the passage, and records which
    references were verified and against which edition — so a reviewer can see
    what was checked rather than only what was found wrong.
  4: >-
    Decides the case where the reference resolves, the clause exists and it
    does not support the claim attached to it — a general clause cited for a
    specific requirement, a note read as normative, or an adjacent subject.
    Defends the finding against a reviewer who has confirmed the reference
    resolves and considers it verified, and states what in the source would
    change the position.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0008` —
do not restate it here.

**`authorityStatus: interpretation`, and this is the first element in the
corpus to use it.** It means what it says: this is the project's reading, and a
reader should weigh it as an argument rather than as a requirement. The three
citations are honest and none of them mentions AI, references generated by a
tool, or anything close. `8.3.2` forbids relying on uncontrolled external
documents and reaches this case by argument, not by wording. Recording that in
data rather than burying it in prose is the point of the field — a reader who
disagrees with the argument can see exactly what they are disagreeing with.

**`volatility: volatile` with an explicit six-month `reviewDue`.** CM-21 is
largely volatile and this element sits in the fastest-moving part of it. The
*competence* is durable — verifying a reference against its source is not a new
skill. What dates is the account in `BOK-0008` §s02 of what the tools do, and
an element whose knowledge refs go stale has a broken refresher path even
though nothing in the element itself changed.

**A `judgment` element, and the L4 case is what makes it one.** At L1 to L3
there is a fact of the matter — the reference either resolves or it does not,
and enough access settles it. At L4 the reference resolves and the question is
whether the clause *supports the claim*, which is a reading of the clause and
is genuinely arguable. Two competent people can differ on whether a general
obligation supports a specific attributed method, and the element assesses the
defence rather than the verdict.

**Item design must not let L4 collapse into L3.** The draw has to be a
reference that genuinely resolves to a genuinely existing clause. If the item
can be settled by looking the clause up and finding it absent, it is testing
L1. The assessable behaviour is holding a position against a reviewer who has
done the resolution check, considers the matter closed, and is not wrong about
the thing they checked.

**Where candidates fail.** Treating resolution as verification — confirming the
document and clause exist and stopping, which is the exact failure the element
names. Checking the references they were handed rather than working out which
claims needed one, so a claim with no reference at all passes unexamined.
Recording the finding without recording the edition consulted, which makes the
verification unrepeatable the moment the standard is revised. Judging by
fluency, and treating a confident passage as better supported. And
over-correcting into refusing every generated reference, which is not
verification either and produces a practitioner who cannot use the tool at all.

**This element describes a gap in the project's own tooling, and that is not an
accident.** `packages/validator/src/checks.ts` verifies that a citation's
`source` appears in `content/sources/registry.yaml`. It cannot check that the
clause exists in that document, and it certainly cannot check that the clause
says what the `relevance` text claims. In the terms of `BOK-0008` §s03, CI
catches the first failure and none of the other three. Every citation in this
corpus — including the three above — rests on a human having read the clause,
and there is nothing in the data recording whether anyone did. Sibling of open
decision 22.
