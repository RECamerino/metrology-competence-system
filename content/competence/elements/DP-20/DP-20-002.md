summary: >-
  What a machine-readable certificate has to carry and why the hard part is
  representing a quantity rather than choosing a file format. The content model
  is fixed by reporting requirements that say nothing about format and are not
  up for redesign; what is being designed is a representation of it. Everything
  a human reader resolves from context — which unit, standard or expanded,
  absolute or relative — has to become explicit, because the consumer has no
  context, and enumerating those is most of the work.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0010` —
do not restate it here.

**The first `emerging` element, and the split inside it is the interesting
part.** The obligations this element rests on are normative and have been for
years; what is emerging is how to represent them so software can act on them.
`authorityStatus` is a single value on the element, so it records the more
cautious of the two — which is right, because a reader needs to know that
parts of what they are learning are not settled. It does mean the field cannot
express *which* parts, and the anchors carry that instead: L1 and L2 sit on the
stable half, L5 sits entirely on the disputed one.

**The first element to set `recertificationMonths`.** Twenty-four rather than
the domain default, because a holder's account of the unsettled half will go
out of date faster than their grasp of the reporting requirements. This is the
field doing exactly what its description says — competence decays at different
rates and a regulatory element is not a physical principle.

**`knowledge` at ceiling 5, and the same caution as `CM-08-038`.** L5 for a
knowledge element is giving an account of a genuinely unsettled question, not
resolving it. An item asking the candidate to *decide* how much uncertainty a
certificate should carry is testing judgment and belongs to `DP-20-009`. The
observable behaviour here is stating both positions as their holders would,
including the adoption-cost argument, which candidates who have only read the
national-institute literature consistently cannot do.

**Where candidates fail.** Treating the subject as a file-format question,
which produces an answer about XML and JSON and misses everything in §s03.
Describing the content model as a design decision, when it is fixed by clauses
that predate the whole discussion. Storing value, unit and uncertainty as three
fields. Treating a unit string as machine-readable. Assuming a signature
establishes that the content is correct, or that the signer was accredited —
it establishes neither. And presenting the disputed half as settled in favour
of the richer model, which is the position most of the published literature
argues for and is not the position most issuing laboratories hold.

**Both L5-bearing knowledge elements in this batch needed the same reframing,
and that is worth someone's attention.** `docs/anchor-template.md` describes L4
and L5 in terms of handling a case the procedure does not cover and resolving
one no approach settles. Those are judgment descriptions. They are writable for
`knowledge` only by substituting *give an account of* for *resolve*, and the
template does not say so.
