summary: >-
  Holding three claims apart that practitioners routinely collapse: what the
  withdrawn standard required, where a laboratory is still obliged to meet it,
  and what the current framework asks instead. The obligation was never
  regulatory — it arrives on a purchase order — so a document's withdrawal and
  a supplier's duty to satisfy it are separate facts, and only one of them
  changed. Teaching a withdrawn requirement as a live one and dismissing a
  contractual one as obsolete are the same error in opposite directions.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0009` —
do not restate it here.

**The first `historical` element in the corpus, and the status is doing real
work.** It tells a reader that what follows describes a requirement that was
once normative and is not now, which is exactly the distinction the area's own
summary names as the failure mode to avoid. An element teaching Z540.3 as
current would be schema-valid and CI-clean; `authorityStatus` is the only field
that prevents it, and it prevents it only for a reader who looks.

**`kind: knowledge` at ceiling 5, which is a shape worth being careful about.**
The anchor template's upper rungs are phrased around handling a case the
procedure does not cover and resolving one no approach settles — which reads as
judgment. For a knowledge element the equivalent is not deciding but
**giving an account**: laying out competing positions in the form their holders
would recognize, saying what turns on each, and saying what would settle it.
That is what the L4 and L5 anchors above ask for, and an item that asks the
candidate to *choose* between the positions is testing `CM-08-039` instead.

**The three claims, and why the element exists.** What the standard required is
a statement about a document. Where it binds is a statement about a purchase
order and cannot be inferred from any measurement. What current practice asks
is a statement about ISO/IEC 17025. Each is separately checkable and they have
different truth conditions, and almost every failure in this area is a
collapse of two of them into one.

**Where candidates fail, in order of frequency.** Presenting 4:1 as what
ISO/IEC 17025 requires, which it never did. Treating withdrawal as ending the
obligation, so a contractual requirement is quietly substituted — the mirror
failure, and the one with commercial consequences. Confusing Z540-1's ratio
with Z540.3's risk requirement, which is easy because Z540.3 retained a
ratio-based compliance route. Describing the risk requirement as merely a
tighter ratio, when it changed the question being asked. And, at L4,
attributing the difference between the two frameworks to stringency rather than
to the assumptions a bare ratio carries.

**A deliberate citation choice, recorded because the element sits next to
`CM-21-012`.** JCGM 106 is cited at its Scope rather than at the clause
covering acceptance intervals and guard bands, because I could not confirm the
subclause numbering without the document open. The scope citation is true,
verifiable and less useful than a precise one; a guessed subclause would have
been more useful and possibly false. That is `BOK-0008` §s05 applied to this
element, and a reviewer with JCGM 106 to hand should replace it with the
precise clause.
