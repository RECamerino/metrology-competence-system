summary: >-
  The categories a technical record has to carry for the measurement to be
  repeatable and the result defensible — item, method, equipment, conditions,
  raw observations, calculation, result, person, date. The requirement is
  written as an outcome rather than a form, which is why it is possible to fill
  in every box on a laboratory's own sheet and still produce a record nobody
  can use. The test is whether a competent stranger could get your answer
  without speaking to you.

Notes for assessors and item authors. The subject is explained in `BOK-0007`
§s02 — do not restate it here.

**A `knowledge` element in an adjacent-competency domain, and both halves of
that matter.** `CM-15` is technical communication, which the corpus carries
because it is a competence metrologists are assessed on in practice and never
formally. That does not make it soft: this element cites a normative clause and
its `authorityStatus` is `normative`, which is worth stating because adjacent
competencies are assumed to be the non-normative ones. Some are. This is not.

**Producing a record is `CM-15-047`; explaining what one must contain is this
element.** The distinction is the usual `knowledge`/`skill` split and it is
easy to blur, because an obvious item shape — "complete this record" — tests
the wrong one. An item here presents a record and asks what it fails to
support.

**The L3 anchor is where the element stops being a checklist.** At L1 and L2
the categories are supplied or recoverable from the document. At L3 there is no
list, and the candidate has to derive what this particular measurement needs
from what a later reader would have to reconstruct — including the entries
that are specific to it and appear on no generic list. An item at L3 that can
be answered from a memorised set of categories is an L2 item.

**Where candidates fail.** Reciting the categories without connecting any of
them to a reader — the requirement is an outcome, and a candidate who cannot
say who is harmed by an omission has not met it. Identifying the item by
description rather than identifier. Treating conditions as optional because the
sheet has no box for them. Omitting raw observations on the grounds that the
calculated result is what the customer wanted, which discards the evidence the
calculation was right. And naming equipment generically, which breaks the one
use of a record nobody anticipates until it is needed: finding every result a
drifting standard touched.

**No prerequisites.** Foundational area, rule 13 — this is where the competence
starts.
