# Rubric — ARC-0002, Diagnose a defective uncertainty budget

Rubric-scored throughout. Double-scored at L4 and L5.

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Defect correctly identified | 30% |
| Direction and approximate magnitude of its effect stated | 20% |
| Correction applied, and correct | 25% |
| Judgements the budget did not record made explicit, with their basis | 25% |

## The diagnosis carries the item

A correctly recomputed number attached to the wrong diagnosis scores **zero on
the first three components**. This is deliberate and reviewers should apply it
without softening: a laboratory that fixes the wrong thing has not improved
its budget, and the arithmetic was never the competence under test.

## Credit for the unresolvable answer

The prompt tells the candidate that "this cannot be resolved from what was
recorded, and here is what I would need" is a valid answer. **It is, and it
scores full marks on the fourth component when it is right.** Some draws are
built so that a contribution genuinely cannot be recovered.

A candidate who invents a plausible value to fill the gap rather than naming
it has demonstrated the opposite of the competence being assessed. Mark that
down even where the invented value is close.

## Level differentiation

Set by `defect_magnitude` in the binding, not by this rubric.

- **L3** — the defect is clear once the budget is read carefully.
- **L4** — the defect is visible only by reasoning about the measurement, not
  by inspecting the table.
- **L5** — the defect sits near the threshold of arguability. A defensible
  case exists that the budget is acceptable as written; the candidate must
  reach and defend a position. At this level, "this is defensible as it
  stands, for these reasons" can score full marks.

## Notes for reviewers

Where the candidate finds a *second*, uninjected defect that is genuinely
present, credit it and report the draw — a generator producing unintended
defects is a bug in the item, not a bonus for the candidate.
